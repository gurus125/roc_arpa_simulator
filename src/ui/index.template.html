Communications (VHF, typed)

Sound signals

Whistle signals of the collision regulations. Rule 34, vessels in sight of one another: 1 short blast, altering to starboard; 2 short, to port; 3 short, astern propulsion; 5 short, in doubt. Rule 35, restricted visibility: 1 prolonged blast when making way, 2 prolonged when under way but stopped, at intervals of not more than 2 minutes. A short blast is about 1 second, a prolonged blast 4 to 6 seconds. The pitch follows the ship's length. Each signal is written in the exercise record.

Parallel index lines (true bearing of the line; distance off, + to starboard of that bearing)

Ahead and astern cut a line short: how far it runs from the point abeam of own ship, along the line and back. Leave them blank for a full line. HDG puts that line parallel to the ship's heading.

Offset EBL: press "EBL origin", then click the point it should start from (it stays at that position). Press again to return it to own ship. Nav lines: press "draw", click the points of a line (a channel edge, a limit), press again to end the line; draw as many lines as needed. They are fixed to the ground, so use them on a ground-stabilised picture. Saved maps are kept on this computer. Off-centring works in relative motion. S band sees through rain and sea clutter better, but small craft and buoys are detected at shorter range and SARTs (and X-band racons) do not answer. Detection ranges follow IMO MSC.192(79) table 2; the band and pulse figures shown are typical values, not those of a particular set.

Tick the EBL or VRM: the line appears and follows the cursor over the display (drag on a touch screen). Double-click or double-tap the display to LOCK it where it is. Use "Move EBL" / "Move VRM" to unlock it again, or fine-tune with the slider (moving the slider also locks it). With neither free, a single click on an echo puts both on it and locks them. The bearing shown is true.

A long pulse gives stronger echoes but poorer range discrimination and a larger minimum range; a short pulse the reverse. Environment

3
80

Refraction changes how far the radar sees over the horizon: in sub-refraction (cold air over a warmer sea, bad weather) targets are detected at about 80% of the usual range; in super-refraction (warm dry air over a cooler sea, settled weather) at up to about 140%. Ducting carries the beam much further and brings second-trace echoes.

The sea comes from the direction of the wind (from the west if no wind is set). A ship with a manoeuvring model rolls, pitches and yaws in it: most roll in a beam sea, most yaw with the sea on the bow or quarter. Heavy rolling tips the radar beam and weakens echoes abeam; yawing smears a head-up picture. The exercise ship is not moved by the sea.

The tidal stream is added to the steady current. It is zero at slack water, strongest about 3.1 hours later, slack again at 6.2 hours and then runs the other way (ebb).

ARPA operation

100

    Standby clears every target and switches the ARPA off. The self-test checks the ARPA's own calculations against test targets with known answers and checks the gyro and log inputs. A bottom-track log measures speed over the ground, so "sea" stabilised true vectors then show motion over the ground. Sound starts after the first click on the page.

    AIS targets

    Sleeping AIS targets closer than the range chosen are activated by themselves. With automatic acquisition on, an AIS target in an acquisition zone is activated too, with a NEW AIS TARGET warning. A dangerous target is always activated. At most 20 targets can be activated: a caution comes at 19, and a further activation is refused with a warning.

    Filter for sleeping targets

    Filter off: every sleeping target is drawn.

    The filter thins out a crowded picture: a sleeping AIS target is not drawn when any criterion that is set says it is of no interest (further off, passing wide or opening, its CPA far ahead, or slow). A class on its own does not filter: it narrows the other criteria. Activated, dangerous and lost targets and the selected target are always drawn, and no single target can be taken off the picture; the list below still shows every target. While a filter is on, "AIS FILTER" and the criteria are written at the top right of the picture, and the AIS key reads AIS FILT.

    Sleeping targets are small triangles. Click a row to activate a target (vector and heading line) and see its data; click again to put it to sleep. A dangerous target is activated automatically.     Vectors

    With this on, a target that does not violate the CPA and TCPA limits is tracked but shows no vector. Its vector comes back, with the alarm, when it violates the limits. Switch it off before planning a manoeuvre.

    Sea: true vectors show courses and speeds through the water, from gyro and log. Ground: select a small, isolated FIXED target (buoy, rock, light) in the list or on the screen, then choose Ground. The ARPA then removes that target's apparent motion (the set and drift) to show motion over the ground. If the reference is lost or fades, a POOR GROUND REFERENCE alarm sounds and sea stabilisation returns unless it is restored.

    A dashed vector is PRELIMINARY: the track has not settled, so its CPA and TCPA are not yet reliable.

    Alarms

    Anchor watchNM

    Anchor watch: at anchor, select a tracked target that you know is stationary (a small isolated mark) and press "On, selected target". A warning is given when it has moved more than the distance set from where it was: own ship, or the target, is dragging.

    An ALARM (a dangerous target) sounds until it is acknowledged, flashes until then, and stays shown until the danger has gone. A WARNING gives one short signal, flashes until acknowledged, and is announced again every 5 minutes if it is left. Alerts are acknowledged one at a time: "Acknowledge next" (and the ALARM ACK key) takes alarms first, then the oldest warning. "Silence" stops the sound for 30 seconds without acknowledging anything.
